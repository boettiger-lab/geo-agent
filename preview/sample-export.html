The map is now displaying the top 30% of US h8 hexes by a combined, equal-weight score of irrecoverable carbon and MOBI species richness, excluding areas already protected under GAP Status 1 or 2.

Here's a summary of the methodology and results:

Methodology

  1. Irrecoverable Carbon (2024): Aggregated from native h9 resolution to h8 by SUMming total carbon (Mg C) per cell
  2. MOBI Species Richness: Used directly at h8 (max species count per cell, range 0–32)
  3. Exclusion: Removed all h8 cells overlapping any PAD-US feature with GAP_Sts = '1' or '2' (permanently protected for biodiversity)
  4. Normalization: Min-max normalized both variables to 0–1 (min = 0 for both; max = 32 for MOBI, 28,342 Mg C for carbon)
  5. Combined score: Equal weight average → (mobi_norm + carbon_norm) / 2
  6. Top 30% filter: Kept cells with combined score ≥ 0.0613 (the 70th percentile)

What the map shows

The viridis color ramp (dark purple → yellow) indicates combined priority score — yellow hexes are the highest-priority cells with both high irrecoverable carbon stocks and high species richness that are not already under strict biodiversity protection (GAP 1/2). These represent potential conservation opportunity areas where both climate and biodiversity values converge.
